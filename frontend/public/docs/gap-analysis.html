Phase 1 · What is already covered

The current material gives a strong view of what is happening in AI answers

The PRD and user stories already cover the measurement and intervention loop: visibility, recommendation, citation, position, competitor gaps, evidence, actions and GEO-level lift.

Source basis: the dashboard user stories define the product as Measure → Diagnose → Prioritize → Act → Re-measure → Prove lift, including KPI drilldown, competitive gaps, actions and intervention lift.

Phase 2 · Where the answer stops

The missing question is not another number. It is what the number means for the business.

The current material can tell us that a GEO signal is weak, that a competitor is ahead, and even produce a recommendation. It does not yet establish whether that signal represents a meaningful business opportunity — or whether the brand actually moved closer to its intended outcome.

1 · WHAT HAPPENED? Measure the number

Example: “Visibility is 42% in India.”

2 · WHERE / FOR WHOM? Understand the gap

Example: “HCP treatment-selection questions are weaker than Competitor B.”

3 · SO WHAT? Understand the business meaning

Example: “Is this an important decision point for this molecule, and would fixing it matter?”

The current product gets us strongly through the first two questions.
The gap I want to explore is the third: what does this mean for the user, the market and the brand?
Question we need to answer What the current material can answer What remains unresolved
Where is the leakage?
 42% visibility, 28% recommendation, competitor B at 51%, citation gap of 12 points. Is this actually a valuable leakage point, or is it a low-demand / low-priority area?
Why does this gap matter?
 The answer can show prompt, intent, journey stage, citations, sources and competitor evidence. Does this represent an important patient/HCP information need or a meaningful decision point?
Why is competitor B winning?
 It can show that B is more visible, recommended or cited. Is B the relevant alternative for this molecule, indication, lifecycle stage, audience and country — and what is giving B the advantage?
What should the recommendation engine recommend?
 It can turn a detected gap into an action and route work to GEO capabilities. What evidence makes this the right action for this business priority, now? What expected effect are we trying to influence?
Did the intervention improve the GEO position?
 Baseline and post-intervention visibility, citation, accuracy and position lift can be measured. Did that GEO movement change anything in the user's decision path or market position?
Did the brand move closer to its business outcome?
 AI Referral Conversion is already a dashboard KPI, and AI-influenced pipeline attribution is listed as a P2 capability. What does the referral represent in the molecule's decision path, what downstream business outcome matters, and what evidence connects the two?
This is the boundary I want to test:
The current product can measure GEO lift. The unresolved question is whether it can explain the meaning of that lift across user behaviour, market context and the brand's business outcome — without claiming causality that the data cannot support.

The gap is broader than business outcome

Reviewing the PRD, user stories and research together, the missing layer is not one feature. Several pieces of context exist as filters or metrics, but the product does not yet define how they become evidence for a business decision.

Dimension What exists today What is still missing as an analytical question
How the numbers connect
 Visibility, citation, recommendation, position, accuracy and trends can be measured and drilled into. How do these numbers connect? For example, when citation rises but recommendation does not, what does that tell us about the brand's position in the answer?
What users are doing
 Intent, journey stage, branded/unbranded prompts and AI Referral Conversion are represented. What are patients/HCPs actually asking? Are they using AI at this point? And did their behaviour change? Prompt configuration is not the same as observed user behaviour.
How the market is changing
 Market and country filters, trends, lifecycle/intent dimensions and a P2 cross-market narrative are present. How is the market changing? Is AI demand growing, shifting to different questions, changing by lifecycle stage, or changing the relevant competitive set? A static market filter does not answer market evolution.
Understanding the competitor
 Competitor visibility, citation share, recommendation rate, answer position and source ownership are measurable. Which competitor really matters here, and why are they doing better? The answer requires molecule × indication × lifecycle × audience × country context.
Why this action?
 The recommendation/action layer can prioritize gaps, assign owners/agents and store expected/actual lift. Why is this the right thing to act on? What business context, evidence and expected downstream effect justify prioritizing it over another gap?
Did the business move?
 AI Referral Conversion is a KPI; AI-influenced pipeline attribution is explicitly a P2 capability. What business result are we trying to move, and what evidence shows the brand moved closer to it? The current MVP definition stops at measured GEO intervention outcomes.
The common pattern: the product has many of the ingredients — metrics, filters, competitors, prompts, recommendations and lift — but the current definition does not explicitly model the relationships between those ingredients and the downstream business decision.

Source basis: the user stories explicitly provide intent/journey-stage taxonomy, market and competitor configuration, GEO KPI drilldown, recommendations/actions and intervention lift. The research adds the unresolved questions around real AI-user demand, contextual competitors, changing AI behaviour and causal limits.

Phase 2 · One example

Same GEO problem. Two very different business decisions.

A low GEO number does not automatically mean “fix it now.” We need to understand what is behind the number before deciding whether it is a real business opportunity.

Example A · A weak number

What we see:
Brand A has 42% visibility in India.

What the dashboard can show:
The prompts where visibility is low, the journey stage, the competitor, the sources and the GEO metrics.

What we still need to know:

  • Are people actually asking these questions often?
  • Is this an important decision point for this molecule?
  • Is India an important market for this brand?
  • Is the competitor winning something that matters?

Possible decision: this may be a low-priority GEO gap.

→

Example B · Same number, different meaning

What we see:
Brand A also has 42% visibility in another market.

But the context is different:
This is a high-priority market. The questions are around treatment selection. The audience is important to the brand. Competitor B is being recommended more often.

Now the questions become:

  • Why is Competitor B winning these answers?
  • What information is the user looking for?
  • Is our brand missing from an important decision point?
  • If we improve this GEO gap, what user or business outcome are we trying to influence?

Possible decision: this could be a high-priority opportunity.

This is the gap:
The number 42% is not enough to tell us which problem deserves attention. The product needs to help connect the GEO number with market importance, user need, competitive position and the business goal.

Why this matters for the recommendation engine

The recommendation engine can already say “fix this GEO gap.”

The deeper question is: “Why should we fix this gap before the other gaps?”

That answer needs evidence about the market, the user, the competitor and the business priority — not just the size of the GEO gap.
CURRENT ANSWER “Where are we weak?”

42% visibility in this market / prompt group / journey stage.

NEXT QUESTION “Why does this weakness matter?”

Is this an important user need, market and competitive opportunity?

BUSINESS QUESTION “What would success change?”

If we fix it, what user behaviour or business result are we trying to move?

The examples are illustrative. The point is to show why the same GEO metric can lead to different priorities when the market, user and business context changes.

Phase 3 · What evidence is missing

To interpret the GEO signal, we need context around it

1 · Market AI demand · market priority · molecule · indication · lifecycle · country · market change
2 · What users are doing Observed AI questions · persona · information need · intent · journey stage · adoption / decision signals
3 · Competitors Relevant alternatives · recommendation position · source authority · why the competitor wins
4 · Did the business move? Referral / engagement → decision-path movement → molecule-specific business measure
RESEARCH SIGNALVisibility ≠ influence

A brand can be cited while another brand is recommended. A visibility number alone does not tell us who is winning the answer.

RESEARCH SIGNALTracked prompts ≠ actual demand

Real-user prompt research raises the question of whether our configured prompt universe reflects what patients and HCPs are actually asking.

RESEARCH SIGNALCompetitor ≠ fixed list

Competitive relevance can change by molecule, indication, lifecycle stage, audience and country.

RESEARCH SIGNALMovement ≠ causality

Observed AI changes can also reflect demand shifts, content changes, model changes and refresh cycles.

BUSINESS QUESTIONUser decision matters

The relevant outcome may sit behind patient choice, HCP choice, access, initiation, adherence or another molecule-specific business measure.

BUSINESS QUESTIONPriority matters

The same GEO gap should not automatically receive the same investment across markets, intents or journey stages.

Important distinction: a dimension being available as a filter does not mean the product answers the business question behind that dimension. “Market = India” is context. “Why did India become a higher/lower priority for this molecule?” is analysis.

What this means for one real example

Observed: Brand A's citation share moves from 22% → 31%.
We can ask: Did this happen on high-demand questions? Did recommendation change? Did the relevant competitor lose position? Did the change occur in a priority market and decision stage? Did the audience behaviour we care about move?

The point: the 9-point citation increase is useful evidence, but it is not yet the business answer.

Phase 5 · The alternative question

Do we need GEO Analytics to answer the whole chain?

Before turning “connect every dot” into a product requirement, there is a more basic question: which part should GEO answer, and which part should come from another system?

Option A · Business / CRM / commercial analytics

Can answer commercial outcomes and customer behaviour when the required client data exists.

Limit: it does not explain the AI answer, prompt, citation or competitor mechanism behind the GEO signal.

Option B · Market / primary research

Can answer user information needs, decision drivers and some behaviour questions.

Limit: it does not continuously observe how AI engines represent the brand and competitors.

Option C · GEO + external business data

GEO owns the AI evidence chain and connects it to the market and business evidence needed downstream.

Question: is this the boundary we actually want?

Why not answer this elsewhere?
Because no single existing layer sees the whole chain. Commercial systems can show outcomes but not the AI mechanism; market research can explain needs and behaviour but not continuously observe AI representation; GEO can observe the AI layer but cannot infer downstream business impact from GEO metrics alone. The product boundary therefore needs an explicit handoff, not an assumed end-to-end claim.
The decision before the solution:
What should GEO Analytics be accountable for explaining, what evidence must come from elsewhere, and where should the handoff occur?

Review conclusion

What I would now call the product gap

Not a missing dashboard metric
The current product already has many of the numbers and views we need. The larger gap is whether those signals can be interpreted as a connected decision system: what changed, why it matters, for whom, against whom, what should happen next, and whether the relevant business outcome moved.
GAP 01Signal → meaning

Metrics exist; the relationship between metrics and the leakage is not yet explicit.

GAP 02Signal → user behaviour

Intent and journey-stage taxonomy exist; observed patient/HCP behaviour and AI adoption at the decision point are not established by the current dashboard definition.

GAP 03Signal → market evolution

Markets can be filtered and compared; changing AI demand, questions, adoption and competitive context are not yet a defined analytical layer.

GAP 04Signal → recommendation

Recommendations exist; the evidence standard for why one opportunity should outrank another needs to be explicit.

GAP 05Signal → business outcome

AI Referral Conversion exists as a KPI; the downstream business chain and attribution boundary remain unresolved.

GAP 06Signal → changing environment

The research shows AI search behaviour is changing quickly; the product needs a way to distinguish a real brand change from a changing measurement environment.
